MIDA / Institutional Structure

Below The Line

MIDA's seven-member board has to disclose its conflicts of interest every year. The people actually running its individual project areas, on five separate subsidiary boards, mostly don't have to disclose anything at all — and MIDA's own website says so, in plain language.

What MIDA's Own Site Says

+

MIDA's board-members page carries two sentences, unremarkable in placement, that describe the entire structure this piece is about:

"Board members of public infrastructure districts are not required to file a disclosure if a district's annual budget is less than $10 million. Board members of development review committees are not required to file a disclosure."

midaut.org/board-members, citing Utah Code §67-16-3(16)

MIDA runs an eight-member board — seven voting, one nonvoting — that does have to disclose. But the board doesn't run the individual project areas directly. It created five separate Public Infrastructure Districts and two Development Review Committees to do that, and populated them with its own people. Those bodies are where the granular decisions actually happen — and per MIDA's own stated rule, most of the people on them don't have to say what else they're connected to.

Same Board, Different Disclosure

+

Harter and Ostermiller hold the identical set of six seats. Their own signed 2026 disclosure forms describe that role two different ways.

Gary Harter
Unpaid

His own words: "Board Member, Military Installation Development Authority and five Public Infrastructure Districts associated with MIDA (unpaid)."

Mike Ostermiller
Paid

His own form describes the same role as paid consulting work, with staff access — not the unpaid volunteer framing Harter uses for the identical seats.

Neither disclosure is hidden or incomplete — both are filed, both are on the record, both describe what appears to be the same structural role. What differs is compensation, disclosed honestly by both men, in their own words, on the same form, for the same board.

Harter's form also answers, in his own sworn words, the question of what the role actually does:

"As a board member, for MIDA, its associated PIDs, the Utah Defense Alliance and the Timpanogos Valley Theater, I participate in oversight of the operations and in the case of MIDA and its five PIDS, I participate in the approval for issuing bonds."

Gary Harter, MIDA 2026 Disclosure Statement, signed 1/26/2026

Gage Froerer

Froerer's disclosure history spans more than a decade of Utah House and Weber County filings. Checked against the same registry, several entities move on and off the record in ways worth laying out plainly.

  • 2018
    Nordic Valley Land Associates LLC (NVLA) first appears on Froerer's Utah House disclosure — named, no ownership percentage given.
  • Nov. 2022
    After a resident pressed the County Attorney and State Auditor, Froerer files a special, one-off disclosure — not a routine annual filing — stating a "16% interest in Nordic Valley Land surrounding existing Ski Resort" through a family trust.
  • 2025
    NVLA appears again, listed plainly on his routine annual Weber County disclosure — the only year reviewed where it shows up in the ordinary form, rather than a special filing.
  • 2026
    NVLA is absent from his disclosure, filed five to eight days before the company's formal dissolution.

His stated 16% trust interest and an earlier-reported 3.3% personal stake are the same underlying interest described two different ways — a 20% share in a family trust that itself holds 16% of NVLA works out to roughly 3.3% of NVLA personally — not a contradiction between two different numbers.

Several of Froerer's other public-body seats — the Utah Counties Indemnity Pool and the Northern Utah Economic Alliance among them — are confirmed, on the record, as roles that come with being the sitting Weber County Commissioner: the full commission voted him into the Indemnity Pool seat in open session, and NUEA's own materials describe its chairmanship as shared between the sitting Weber and Davis county commissioners. Those are a different kind of finding from the entities above and aren't treated the same way here.

The Governor's Own Reversal

+

None of this happened without the Governor's knowledge. Gov. Spencer Cox's own public statements, tracked across eight months, form a complete arc from personally soliciting the project to disclaiming any role in it at all.

  • Late 2025
    Per Kevin O'Leary's own account, Cox met with him alongside Speaker Schultz and Senate President Adams. O'Leary's paraphrase of their side of the conversation: "We want to build this thing, can you do it?"
  • Jan 2026
    Reported as having "initially backed the project" from this meeting on.
  • Late Apr 2026
    At his monthly press conference, Cox fiercely defends the project and dismisses caution about it directly: "I'm so tired of our country taking years to get stuff done — it's the dumbest thing ever... We think that taking time makes things better or safer."
  • May 4, 2026
    Box Elder Commissioners approve; public backlash intensifies statewide.
  • May 29, 2026
    Cox signs an executive order in response to "statewide outrage" — after, as one critic publicly asked, he'd spent weeks "singing its praises."
  • Aug 20, 2026
    Full retreat: "not a decision made by me," years of claimed prior worry about MIDA, and — the same week — he says he'd require the exact kind of cautious, closed-loop cooling oversight he'd mocked in April, "if I felt I could do it with executive authority."

Stated plainly: in January, his own side asked the investor "can you do it?" In April, he called caution about the project "the dumbest thing ever." By August, he says the decision "was not made by me," claims years of quiet concern about the very authority that made it, and wishes he had the power to impose the same caution he'd dismissed four months earlier.
