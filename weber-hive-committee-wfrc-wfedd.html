Wasatch Front Regional Council · Committee Structure

Two Councils, One Building

Who sits on the Wasatch Front Regional Council and the Wasatch Front Economic Development District, how the private seats get filled, and where the money goes.

How to read this

  1. WFRC is the region's transportation and growth-planning authority for six counties — almost entirely sitting mayors and county commissioners.
  2. WFEDD is a separate committee that operates "under the auspices of" WFRC. Federal rules require it to include private-sector seats — banking, real estate, construction — alongside the government members. That's the layer worth watching.
  3. WFEDD's real power is narrow but specific: it decides which local projects get written into the region's federally-required economic strategy, the document that determines who can compete for EDA grant money.

What stands out

SAME PERSON, BOTH SEATS

Gage Froerer chairs both

Froerer sits on the full WFRC Council as a Weber County Commissioner, and separately co-chairs the WFEDD Strategy Committee — the body that decides which projects make it into the region's federal economic strategy. Froerer's real estate holdings, including undisclosed interests connected to the Nordic Valley development, are documented elsewhere on this site.

A FORMAL SEAT, NOT JUST CONSULTING WORK

Zions Bank sits on WFEDD directly

Shaleane Gee holds a named Member seat on WFEDD for Zions Bank. Zions Public Finance is documented elsewhere on this site playing as many as seven different roles across Utah Public Infrastructure District deals — feasibility study author, financial advisor, bond underwriter, direct bond purchaser, trustee, compliance monitor, and special assessment administrator — and a separate Zions employee sits on the state commission that reviews PID bond issuances. Here, Zions has an institutional seat on the committee that decides which projects get access to federal development money.

REAL ESTATE, ACTIVELY VOTING

Chris Sloan isn't a name on paper

Sloan, a real estate broker and past president of the Utah Association of Realtors, is formally listed as an alternate — but committee minutes show him attending meetings and personally seconding motions, including the vote to ratify the committee's own membership for the year.

Where the money goes

WFRC administers several funding programs. Most of the money is transportation-construction financing not yet examined here. The two programs reviewed in detail are smaller but tell different stories.

Program Scale What it funds
CDBG Small Cities ~$1.17M (2026) Small-town infrastructure — water lines, sewer rehab, main street reconstruction. Weber, Tooele, Morgan counties, population under 50,000.
Transportation & Land Use Connection (TLC) ~$1.9M/year, 15 projects (2026) Technical assistance for city general plans, zoning, and land-use studies — the planning work that shapes where development happens next.
Station Area Planning $790K (first round, 2022) Required plans for cities with a rail or transit station, under state law HB462.
EDA Partnership Planning Grant $420,000 (2023-2026) WFEDD's own core federal operating funds — not a project grant, the money that keeps the committee running.

CASE IN POINT

Ogden Valley City's TLC-funded plan

Ogden Valley City — already covered on this site for a 512.6% emergency property tax vote — received a 2026 TLC award to write its founding general plan, transportation master plan, and zoning roadmap. The plan is being written by JUB Engineering, an outside consulting firm with its own record of securing large WFRC grants for other client cities. The document itself is, almost word for word, the 2016 Weber County general plan — whose own credited consultant team included Zions Bank Public Finance, and whose planning commission included Steve Waldrip, now the Governor's senior housing adviser.
